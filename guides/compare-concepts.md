# ⇄ Compare two concepts

Some pairs trip everyone up: `merge` and `rebase`, `LEFT JOIN` and `INNER JOIN`, precision and recall, TCP and UDP. **Compare two concepts** puts any two side by side and has AI explain what actually separates them, in the same clear structure every time.

## How to pick the two

On any cheat sheet, the **Compare two concepts with AI** bar sits just above the Quick Index. It has one card per concept:

- **Type to find a concept.** Search this sheet by name, example or description, and typos are fine.
- **Or look beyond this sheet.** Each card has its own **This sheet / All cheat sheets** switch. Choose All, and you can narrow that side by **category** and **subcategory**, so you can compare, say, a join in SQL with one in pandas.
- **Or pick straight from the page.** Press **Compare rows** in the Quick Index bar to get a checkbox on every concept there, press ⇄ beside any table heading to pick rows in the tables, or press **Compare with another row** under a row's own explanation.

Whichever way you pick, the choice shows up everywhere at once. Then press **Explain the difference**. Press **Esc** when you're done comparing.

## Not sure what to compare it with?

Pick one concept and a small map opens under the other card: your concept in the middle, the ones closest to it in meaning around it. The nearer a concept sits to the middle, and the thicker its line, the closer the two are. Click any of them and it fills the empty card, just as if you'd searched for it. You can still type your own instead.

- **The card's switch decides where it looks.** On **This sheet**, the map shows the closest concepts on the sheet you're reading. On **All cheat sheets**, it looks across the whole library (a few from each sheet, so one sheet can't fill it), and your category and subcategory choice narrows it.
- **Colours tell you where each one lives:** one colour for this sheet, another for other sheets, with the sheet's name under the concept. Concepts on sheets outside your plan are greyed out with a lock and can't be picked.
- **It works from meaning, not matching words**, so it finds the concepts that are easy to mix up even when they're named nothing alike. A concept added very recently may not be on the map yet; search for it instead.

The map needs you to be signed in.

## What you get, every time

1. **The difference in one sentence**, right at the top.
2. **What each one is**, in a line each.
3. **Side by side:** a small table comparing them on the things that matter for that pair: what each is for, what it works on and produces, when it runs, what it costs, where it lives.
4. **What really separates them:** the reasons behind those differences, and what they lead to.
5. **One case, both ways:** the same real situation run through both, with code when the examples are code.
6. **When to reach for which**, each with a concrete situation.
7. **Watch out:** the classic mix-up, and what it costs you.

Not every pair is a choice between two options. When the two **work together** (a planning meeting and a work-in-progress limit, an idea and the tool that does it), the same sections show how they fit and a case where both play their part. When they're simply **unrelated** or only share a name, it says so plainly and shows each in its own world, rather than inventing a contrast.

## Why it's different from asking a chatbot

- **It starts from our checked, sourced cheat sheets.** The explanation works from the two concepts as they're written there, with their examples, not from whatever a model remembers.
- **Same structure, every time**, so you can scan a comparison in seconds and compare comparisons.
- **Commands shown the way they're really used.** Code appears only when the concept's own example is code, and it stays close to that example: no made-up functions, no pretend code for something that isn't code.
- **Honest about odd pairs.** If two things aren't really alternatives, you're told.

## Plans and limits

Comparing uses the same allowance as every other **[AI Explanation](ai-explanations.md)**: each new comparison uses one explanation, and opening it again later is free (you'll find it in the panel's History). Searching all cheat sheets needs you to be signed in, and concepts from sheets outside your plan show a lock and can't be compared, since the comparison reads their full text.
