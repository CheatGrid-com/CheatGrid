# AI Explanations

Our content is written to be scanned and drilled. That's the job: a cheat-sheet row gives you a concept in one line, a flashcard gives you the shortest thing you can recall, a practice question gives you four options. All of it assumes you already understand.

Sometimes you don't, and then you're stuck. That's what **Explain** is for. Tap it and you get a plain-language walkthrough written for someone meeting the subject for the first time, with a real example instead of an abstract one.

It's on cheat sheets, flashcards, practice tests, certifications, roadmaps, cookbook recipes, Life Skills pages, the Concept Maps and My Vault, and it always explains **whatever it's sitting next to**.

## Where to find it

Look for the small ✨ button. It's icon only, so it stays out of the way, and it sits next to whatever it explains. Two carry their name because they answer one specific question: **Summarize** at the top of a cheat sheet, and **Why was my answer wrong?** after a wrong answer in a practice test:

**On cheat sheets**
- **Summarize, at the top:** the one labelled button, in the Quick Index bar. It reads the whole topic, every table and every concept, and gives you the shape of the subject: what it is, the few ideas everything else rests on, how the areas fit together, one real worked scenario, and what usually trips people up. It's the fastest way to decide whether a topic is what you need, and a good way to come back to one you half remember. It's a bit longer than the others, because it's covering a whole subject.
- **Beside a table heading:** the map of that whole area. What it's for, how the entries group together, how to choose between them, and one worked scenario. Use it when you open a section and don't know what you're looking at.
- **On every row, two buttons:** the ✨ one goes deep on that concept (what it is in a line, how it works, a real example with real values, when you'd use it, and any trap). The 🌐 one answers a completely different question: **where is this actually used?** Real settings, one scenario told start to finish with real numbers, what breaks when you get it wrong, and the place it turns up that you'd never guess. That last part is usually the bit people remember.
- **Compare two concepts with AI:** pick any two concepts, from this sheet or any other, and get the difference explained in the same structure every time: the difference in a sentence, a side-by-side table, one real case run through both, when to reach for which, and the classic mix-up. Start from the bar above the Quick Index. All the details are in **[Compare two concepts](compare-concepts.md)**.
- **On the free preview of a locked topic:** the rows you can see there get the buttons too. You don't need the subscription to have the preview explained to you, it just uses your own allowance like anywhere else.

**On flashcards**
- **While studying:** next to **End**. Stuck on the card in front of you? It unpacks what the back actually means and why it's true, without leaving your session.
- **In Browse:** on every card, and beside every section heading (the section one tells you what that group is about and which cards to understand first).
- **Choosing tables in Practice:** beside each table, so you can see what a group covers before you drill it.

**On practice tests**
- **While practising:** next to Continue. It teaches the idea behind the question and, most usefully, why each wrong option is wrong.
- **Why was my answer wrong?:** appears under the options when you pick a wrong one. It starts from YOUR answer: why it looks right, the exact words in the question that rule it out, what the right answer does instead, and one quick check to use next time. It's also in the Mistake notebook's retest.
- **In Browse:** on every question, and beside each test heading.
- **On the test list:** what that test covers, before you start.
- **Never in Exam mode.** An exam is meant to be faced cold, so the buttons aren't there.

**On certifications:** exactly the same as practice tests, and still in plain language. Exam prep doesn't have to mean dense exam-speak.

**On roadmaps**
- **At the top:** what the field is really about, why the steps come in that order, and what the work actually involves.
- **Beside each section:** the same for that one phase.

**On cookbook recipes:** at the top for the whole recipe, and beside each step when you're stuck on that one.

**On Life Skills pages:** for the whole page, and beside its groups, lines, checklist items and myths.

**On the Concept Maps:** **Explain this link**, when you open the link between two sheets (press **↔** next to a partner). It tells you how the two fit together: which part of the idea each piece of the tool handles, one real case walked through step by step, what the tool still leaves up to you, and which rows in each sheet to read next. It only uses the rows you can see. Inside that window every matched pair of rows has its own **Explain this pair** too: is it really the same thing, the idea on its own, then how the tool does it, with a small example you can try and how to check it worked (say, **Broadcast Join** in Big Data beside **Broadcast joins** in Databricks).

**In My Vault:** on every saved row and on each saved topic's table.

Every one of them opens the same panel, and you can keep the page visible behind it.

## What makes these different

Every explanation is written to the same rules:

- **Plain words.** Jargon gets explained the moment it appears, in the same sentence. No assumed background.
- **Real examples, not placeholders.** Actual numbers, actual filenames, actual values, with the result shown. `17 // 5 -> 3` teaches something; `x // y` doesn't.
- **Short and structured.** Bullets and short sections, never a wall of text. Long enough to actually land, short enough to read.
- **The general rule at the end.** A concrete example on its own doesn't transfer to the next problem, so each explanation steps back to the principle it demonstrates.
- **No filler.** It starts with the answer and stops when it's finished. No "great question", no summary of what it just said.

Each place has its own way of teaching, because the useful answer is different: a flashcard explanation unpacks the card, a practice question explanation goes after the wrong options, a roadmap explanation is about the field rather than the list of topics, and a topic summary draws the map of a whole subject instead of walking its tables.

**Real-world use has one extra rule**, and it's the one that keeps it worth reading: it isn't allowed to make things up to sound impressive. No invented companies, no invented statistics, no "everyone does it this way" when they don't. If it can't point at something real, it describes the actual work instead of borrowing a famous name. A specific unnamed scenario beats a made-up case study every time.

One thing worth knowing about the summary: our tables are ordered on purpose, foundational first and specialised last, and the rows inside each table run from what people use daily down to the rare corners. The summary reads that order and weights itself the same way, so the things you actually need get the words.

## Reopening is free

Once a row or table is explained, that explanation is saved in your browser. Open it again and it appears instantly and doesn't count against your allowance. If you'd rather have a fresh take, **Regenerate** writes a new one (that one does count).

You can also close the panel while it's still writing. The explanation keeps going in the background, so when you open that row again it's finished and waiting for you. It still only counts once.

Explanations are saved on the device you're using, and clear when you sign out.

## Finding one again, and sizing the panel

The **History** button at the top of the panel lists everything you've had explained, newest first. Click any entry to read it again. Nothing is regenerated and nothing is charged: it's the text you already have.

You can also drag any edge or corner of the panel to resize it. The size you pick is remembered for every explanation after that, so set it once and it stays. Double-click an edge to go back to the default.

## Worth knowing

The explanations are written by an AI reading our cheat sheet content, and AI can get things wrong. The source table is right there next to it, so check anything that matters.

**If an answer is wrong, unsafe or just off, press Report** at the bottom of the panel. Say in a line what's wrong with it and send: the answer itself and the page it came from travel with your report, so we can see exactly what you saw. The assistant has the same Report under each of its answers. You can also write to **[hello@cheatgrid.com](mailto:hello@cheatgrid.com)** about a row that's confusing in the cheat sheet itself: bad rows get fixed.
