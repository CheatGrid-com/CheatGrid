# AI Explanations

Our content is written to be scanned and drilled. That's the job: a cheat-sheet row gives you a concept in one line, a flashcard gives you the shortest thing you can recall, a practice question gives you four options. All of it assumes you already understand.

Sometimes you don't, and then you're stuck. That's what **Explain** is for. Tap it and you get a plain-language walkthrough written for someone meeting the subject for the first time, with a real example instead of an abstract one.

It's on cheat sheets, flashcards, practice tests, certifications, roadmaps and My Vault, and it always explains **whatever it's sitting next to**.

## Where to find it

Look for the small ✨ button. It's icon only, so it stays out of the way, and it sits next to whatever it explains:

**On cheat sheets**
- **Beside a table heading:** the map of that whole area. What it's for, how the entries group together, how to choose between them, and one worked scenario. Use it when you open a section and don't know what you're looking at.
- **On every row:** deep on that one concept. What it is in a line, how it works, a real example with real values, when you'd use it, and any trap.

**On flashcards**
- **While studying:** next to **End**. Stuck on the card in front of you? It unpacks what the back actually means and why it's true, without leaving your session.
- **In Browse:** on every card, and beside every section heading (the section one tells you what that group is about and which cards to understand first).
- **Choosing tables in Practice:** beside each table, so you can see what a group covers before you drill it.

**On practice tests**
- **While practising:** next to Continue. It teaches the idea behind the question and, most usefully, why each wrong option is wrong.
- **In Browse:** on every question, and beside each test heading.
- **On the test list:** what that test covers, before you start.
- **Never in Exam mode.** An exam is meant to be faced cold, so the buttons aren't there.

**On certifications:** exactly the same as practice tests, and still in plain language. Exam prep doesn't have to mean dense exam-speak.

**On roadmaps**
- **At the top:** what the field is really about, why the steps come in that order, and what the work actually involves.
- **Beside each section:** the same for that one phase.

**In My Vault:** on every saved row and on each saved topic's table.

Every one of them opens the same panel, and you can keep the page visible behind it.

## What makes these different

Every explanation is written to the same rules:

- **Plain words.** Jargon gets explained the moment it appears, in the same sentence. No assumed background.
- **Real examples, not placeholders.** Actual numbers, actual filenames, actual values, with the result shown. `17 // 5 -> 3` teaches something; `x // y` doesn't.
- **Short and structured.** Bullets and short sections, never a wall of text. Long enough to actually land, short enough to read.
- **The general rule at the end.** A concrete example on its own doesn't transfer to the next problem, so each explanation steps back to the principle it demonstrates.
- **No filler.** It starts with the answer and stops when it's finished. No "great question", no summary of what it just said.

Each place has its own way of teaching, because the useful answer is different: a flashcard explanation unpacks the card, a practice question explanation goes after the wrong options, and a roadmap explanation is about the field rather than the list of topics.

## Reopening is free

Once a row or table is explained, that explanation is saved in your browser. Open it again and it appears instantly and doesn't count against your allowance. If you'd rather have a fresh take, **Regenerate** writes a new one (that one does count).

You can also close the panel while it's still writing. The explanation keeps going in the background, so when you open that row again it's finished and waiting for you. It still only counts once.

Explanations are saved on the device you're using, and clear when you sign out.

## Finding one again, and sizing the panel

The **History** button at the top of the panel lists everything you've had explained, newest first. Click any entry to read it again. Nothing is regenerated and nothing is charged: it's the text you already have.

You can also drag any edge or corner of the panel to resize it. The size you pick is remembered for every explanation after that, so set it once and it stays. Double-click an edge to go back to the default.
