# Roadmaps

A roadmap is a learning path built from **cheat sheets and their flashcard decks**, ordered into a real sequence for a goal instead of a pile of topics you have to figure out yourself. Pick one for a career, a single skill, or a life goal, and we lay out the steps: read the cheat sheet, review the deck, move on.

## Picking one

Open **Roadmaps** from the top of the site. The page starts by asking what you're here for: **Careers**, **Skills**, or **Life**, with a row of popular ones underneath and then the full list, every roadmap grouped by topic area, so you can browse instead of choosing if you'd rather. Picking a group narrows the page to it. Each card shows the step count, roughly how many hours it takes, and the level, so you know what you're signing up for before you start. Already started something? A **Continue** button near the top jumps you straight back in.

## Seeing the whole path at once

Open a roadmap and it lays out in sections, each with its step cards in order. On a wide screen a slim panel on the right, **The path**, lists every section and every topic in one place, with a tick beside the ones you've finished. Click a topic to jump to it, and it keeps up with you as you scroll, so a long roadmap never leaves you wondering where you are.

## The two bars: progress and memory

Sign in and every roadmap shows two bars: one for what you've worked through, one for what you still remember.

- **Progress** is the simple one: how many required steps you've finished, plus roughly how many hours are left.
- **Memory** is the one that makes a roadmap more than a checklist. It shows how much of the roadmap's flashcards you'd actually get right if quizzed right now, dropping naturally as spaced repetition predicts you're forgetting and climbing back when you review. Low memory turns the bar amber with a one-click link to fix it.

## Ticking steps, automatically or by hand

Read a cheat sheet for a few minutes, or study enough of its flashcards, and the step ticks itself, with a quick notification and an **Undo** if it got it wrong. Prefer more control? A setting lets you require flashcard study before a step ticks, or turn off auto-tick entirely and mark everything by hand. Either way, you can always open a step's **⋯** menu for **Mark done** or **Skip this step** (skip is for things you already know; it still moves you forward).

## Sections, milestones, and the badge

Every roadmap splits into a few sections, each ending in a milestone worth XP once every step in it is resolved. Finish the whole roadmap and you earn its badge, worth more XP and a short celebration. Being honest here matters: the badge asks for most of your steps to be **genuinely** done, not skipped, so skip everything and you'll finish the map but the badge card will tell you what's still missing.

## Keeping your memory up: Review Run and Core Sprint

When cards are due, a **Review Run** panel drops you into a real study session in one click, no deck picker, no setup. Finish it and you're back on the roadmap watching your memory bar move. Lower on the page, a **Core Sprint** walks you through just the foundational cards across every step, one at a time, the fast way to get a real feel for the whole roadmap.

## Following along while you read

Open a cheat sheet that's part of a roadmap and a slim banner up top shows exactly where you are: which roadmap, which step, and quick links to the step before and after it. Signed in, the same banner lets you mark the step done without leaving the article.

## Where roadmaps show up

The homepage has its own Roadmaps section, opening with a "pick up where you left off" strip if you've already started one. Your **[Stats](stats.md)** page has a Roadmaps tab listing every one you've started with its progress, memory, and badge. Roadmaps also show up in the site's own search, tagged so you can tell them apart from a cheat sheet at a glance.

## Free vs. subscription

A roadmap is always free to view and free to track, signed in or not. What changes by plan is the steps inside it, the same cheat sheets, **[flashcards](intelligent-flashcards.md)**, and **[practice tests](practice-tests.md)** that are free or paid everywhere else on the site. A locked step still shows on the map, it just points you to subscribe when you open it.

Progress you build earns **[XP](xp-and-levels.md)** the same way everything else does, and every touch point counts toward your **[Stats](stats.md)**.
